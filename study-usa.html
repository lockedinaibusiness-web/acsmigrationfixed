What to know
Studying in the United States
The US system is broad and flexible — here is how it actually works.

Degree structure

Bachelor's degrees typically take four years, with a liberal-arts core before you specialise in a major. Master's programmes usually run one to two years, and PhDs four to six years with coursework followed by research.

Standardised tests

Most undergraduate applicants submit the SAT or ACT; graduate programmes commonly require the GRE or GMAT. Requirements vary widely by institution, so check each programme directly.

Applications & essays

US applications ask for more than grades — personal statements, activity lists and recommendation letters are standard. Deadlines cluster around early November for January entry.

Student visa (F-1)

You need an I-20 from your university before booking an F-1 visa interview. Bring your acceptance letter, financial documents and academic records.

Work after studying

Curricular Practical Training (CPT) lets you work during your course; Optional Practical Training (OPT) allows up to 12 months of work after graduation, extended to 24–36 months for STEM degrees.

Costs & funding

Tuition varies enormously between public and private institutions. Merit scholarships, graduate assistantships (teaching/research roles that cover tuition plus a stipend) and need-based aid are the main funding routes.
