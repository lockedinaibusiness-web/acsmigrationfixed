What to know
Studying in Germany
Low tuition, strong engineering and research, and a clear post-study work route.

Tuition

Most public universities charge no tuition fees — you pay only a semester contribution (roughly €150–€350) that usually includes public transport. Some private and certain non-EU programmes do charge fees.

Degree structure

Bachelor's programmes typically take three years, master's two years. German-taught programmes generally require the Studienkolleg foundation year if your school qualification isn't directly recognised.

Language & APS

English-taught programmes need IELTS or TOEFL; German-taught ones need TestDaF or DSH. Indian applicants usually also need an APS certificate verifying their academic documents.

Student visa

You need admission confirmation, a blocked account (currently about €11,904) or a sponsor, plus health insurance. Apply at the German mission well before your intake.

Work after studying

You can work up to 140 full days per year alongside your studies, and apply for an 18-month post-study job-seeking residence permit after graduating.

Strong fields

Engineering, automotive, mechanical sciences, computer science, natural sciences and manufacturing technology are Germany's best-known strengths.
